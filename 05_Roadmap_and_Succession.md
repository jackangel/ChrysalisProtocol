## 1. What this document is for

Two questions that proposals in this field rarely answer honestly:

1. **What actually happens, in what order, funded by what?** (§2–§5)
2. **What if the founder dies, quits, or is compromised?** (§3–§4 and §6–§7)

The second question is the more important one. A project that cannot survive its founder's absence is an extension of its founder's will, whatever it claims to be. Chrysalis is designed so that the founder is the most replaceable part.

## 2. The phases

Month 0 is the close of the Tier 1 campaign (the sunset clock in §3 runs from the earlier of campaign close or legal-wrapper registration; Tier 1 funds are raised before the wrapper registers, so campaign close is the anchor in practice). Year 2 begins at month 12, so Phase 2 (months 12–36) deliberately overlaps the end of Phase 1 (months 9–24), and Phase 3 (months 24–60) overlaps the end of Phase 2.

## 2. The Strategic Imperative and Phases

The original assumption—that Chrysalis could be piloted at leisure over several years in a single small municipality—has been superseded by the accelerating velocity of frontier AI and robotics. Testing a local planning question in one town will not protect humanity when labor's economic value goes negative, nor does it address cross-border capital flight or state-level cyber-capture.

We are running out of time. The mission for Year 1 is therefore focused on two non-negotiable imperatives:
1. **Prove the System Is Unattackable:** Engineer the core protocol and prove mathematically, cryptographically, and empirically that it **cannot be corrupted or captured by actors with secret agendas**—whether financial oligarchs, intelligence agencies, corporate AI cartels, or corrupt insiders.
2. **Present the Thesis to the World & Mobilize Sovereign Governments on Universal High Income (UHI):** Launch an international diplomatic and economic offensive. Put the thesis directly before heads of state, finance ministries, international bodies, and the global public to build multilateral coordination on an Automation Dividend before the closed loop is complete.

Month 0 is the close of the Tier 1 campaign.

### Phase 0 — Attack-Proof Architecture & Global Presentation (months 0–12)

**Purpose:** Build the core unhackable engine, subject it to brutal adversarial attack, present the thesis on the world stage, and initiate sovereign coordination on Universal High Income.

**Work.**
1. **Global Presentation of the Thesis:** Launch an international diplomatic and public campaign. Present the Chrysalis framework to international forums (UN, OECD, EU bodies), forward-looking sovereign ministries of finance and labor, and the global public.
2. **Core Protocol Engineering:** Build the open-source reference engine implementing cryptographic sortition lotteries (verifiable randomness via drand/VRF), dual-chain immutable ledger anchoring, and the multi-model adversarial AI deliberation harness (cross-verifying rival model families with open weights and published prompts).
3. **Adversarial Red-Team Challenge & Bounty Program:** Instead of friendly academic review, commission an aggressive, open, paid red-team program. Invite the world’s top cryptographers, security researchers, and game theorists to attempt attacking the system: attempting to rig sortition draws, deanonymize or bribe Guardians, poison AI moderation, or capture the citizen agenda.
4. **Multilateral UHI Treaty Framework & Macro-Modeling:** Publish formal macro-econometric modeling of the Universal High Income Floor, Target, and cross-border automation levies (compute and capital dividends), demonstrating how international coordination eliminates tax-haven leakage.
5. **Legal Wrapper with Immutable Founder Sunset:** Register the non-profit foundation with statutory asset-locks, open-source covenants, and the binding legal clause terminating founder executive power at month 24.
6. **On-Ledger Public Ledger:** Deploy the public expenditure ledger from day one; every single euro spent is verifiable on-chain.

**Exit criteria:** Core protocol software published; formal adversarial red-team audit reports published with zero critical capture vulnerabilities; multilateral UHI treaty draft and econometric model published; thesis presented to at least 10 sovereign governments or international institutions; foundation registered with irrevocable founder sunset.

### Phase 1 — Global Deliberation Testnet & Sovereign Working Group (months 9–24)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate unhackable, sovereign citizen deliberation across borders and convene the first international working group on Universal High Income.

**Work.**
1. **The Global Deliberation Testnet:** Convene an international, multi-lingual sortition assembly (n≈200–500 citizens from participating nations) operating on the audited protocol stack to deliberate and propose amendments to the Model Multilateral UHI Treaty.
2. **Sovereign Working Group on Post-Labor UHI:** Convene the first formal summit of pioneer governments, sovereign wealth funds, and economic institutions to negotiate multilateral automation dividend mechanisms.
3. **Formal Mathematical Verification:** Complete automated formal verification of smart contracts, voting tally proofs (mix-net zero-knowledge proofs), and Guardian consensus protocols.
4. **Interim Guardians Seated:** Cryptographically draw the first 7-member interim Guardian panel from verified global citizen registries to oversee protocol integrity and founder phase-out.
5. **Founder Sunset Handover:** Execute the mechanical termination of founder authority at month 24, rotating all admin keys and governance to sortition trustee panels.

**Exit criteria:** Successful execution of the Global Deliberation Testnet with published cryptographic receipts; convening of the Sovereign UHI Working Group; complete zero-vulnerability formal verification report; founder powers permanently expired on schedule.

### Phase 2 — Sovereign Ratification & Treaty Adoption (years 2–3)

**Purpose:** Move from diplomatic working groups to the first sovereign enactments of the Chrysalis model statutes and UHI treaty instruments.

**Work.**
1. Enactment of Chrysalis Starter Laws (L1–L3, L6 Anti-Capture) by pioneer sovereign or regional governments.
2. Establishment of the first multilateral Automation Dividend pool among participating nations.
3. First full global sortition assembly convenes to review protocol operations.

### What no phase contains

No phase includes building or deploying superhuman AI, handing sovereign authority to machines, or private backroom negotiation. The Bridge gates ([01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §7) remain the only constitutional path for any task delegation, requiring unanimous safety clearance and a supermajority public referendum.

## 3. The founder sunset

**The rule ([01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §3b):** 24 months after the earlier of campaign close or legal-wrapper registration, all founder roles auto-expire. Not announced, not voted, not negotiated: expired, by statute and by code. The founder is salaried only for the 12 funded months of the launch year (§5, line 10).

**Mechanics.**
1. The legal wrapper's statutes fix the expiry date at registration; amendment is barred before the first full assembly convenes and afterwards requires sortition-assembly approval (in which the founder does not sit).
2. Ledger access keys rotate on expiry, to the stewardship council or, at month 24, to the trustee panel (§4); founder keys derive from a time-locked schedule, not from the founder's continued possession.
3. From month 24 onward, the founder is what any citizen is: a voice in the objection protocol, a name in the registry, eligible for sortition like anyone else.
4. If the founder resists expiry, that is the Guardians' first real case ([01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §8, V5b: renunciation theater and its inverse, clinging theater, are both audit triggers).

**Why 24 months.** Long enough to launch (Phases 0–1), short enough that no personality cult can form around the launch window. The founder's only legitimate job is to be the protocol's first user, and to make himself unnecessary.

## 4. Succession — what the protocol does when the founder is gone

The protocol has no succession-of-leaders problem because it has no leaders. It has a succession-of-*function* design that runs on random selection.

**If the founder dies, steps down, or is removed (by the Guardians' referral and a 60% vote of an ad hoc assembly) before month 24:**
1. A **stewardship council** is drawn by cryptographic sortition from the public registry (before sovereign registries integrate, from an open volunteer registry of verified individuals kept by the fiscal host under the published draw procedure): 9 people, stratified like any cohort, serving 6-month staggered terms; the draw is triggered by a public vacancy notice from the Guardians (before they exist, from the fiscal host's board), and a vacant seat is refilled by the same body and procedure.
2. The council holds only the founder's launch duties (publish, procure, spend per the published budget, schedule petitions mechanically under [01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §3 step 1), never policy authority and never power to amend the protocol.
3. The council's only non-administrative acts are to schedule the first full sortition assembly early, completing the transition the founder would have completed at month 24, and to draw the interim Guardians where none exist yet.
4. Every council act is on-ledger; the Guardians audit it like any other organ. Until the Guardian body is fully constituted, the interim Guardians described in [01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §2 perform this audit; before any exist, the fiscal host's independent board does.

**At month 24 (normal case):**
1. The first full sortition assembly convenes (n≈500, stratified, paid).
2. The assembly takes over oversight of the launch duties (procurement and spending pass to the trustee panel in item 3) and exercises the assembly powers defined in [01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §3 (objective function, prosecutor appointment, amendment review, emergency renewal, ledger migration); the launch body (founder or council) held none of those powers, which before then can be exercised only by an ad hoc assembly for a single question.
3. Between assemblies, the legal person's spending, procurement and keys are held by a **trustee panel** of 5 members drawn by sortition from the public registry by the assembly, serving 12 months; the panel acts only on the published budget, signs nothing outside it, and is audited by the Guardians. Ledger signing keys rotate to the panel at month 24.
4. From that point, succession is the ordinary rotation machinery of Organs 1 and 3: sortition draws, term limits, no renewal.

**Why cryptographic random selection (and not election or appointment).**
- Random selection cannot be campaigned for, purchased, or inherited.
- A verifiable draw (public randomness beacon, published commitment, auditable selection; [04_Technical_Specification.md](04_Technical_Specification.md) §4) makes "who continues the work" a personnel decision that is very hard to rig.
- If the founder is gone, *multiple randomly selected people* continue the work, not a designated heir, a board, or the loudest contributor.

## 6. Failure and wind-down paths

The roadmap plans for failure as a first-class outcome:

1. **Systemic vulnerability proven:** If adversarial red-teamers demonstrate that the sortition, ledger, or AI deliberation can be successfully corrupted or hijacked by covert agendas, the finding is published in full. If no cryptographic or architectural mitigation exists, the protocol halts and dissolves. Unspent funds are returned pro rata to backers or transferred on-ledger to a designated civic charity.
2. **Global UHI diplomatic rejection:** If sovereign governments universally refuse to engage with the Multilateral UHI Treaty framework, all econometric data and treaty drafts are published as open public goods, and the protocol focuses exclusively on voluntary bottom-up civic adoption.
3. **Funding shortfall:** If Tier 1 is missed, pledges are returned pro rata. If Tier 2 is not funded within 12 months of Tier 1, unspent funds wind down.
4. **Founder capture or interference:** Founder powers expire mechanically at month 24. Any resistance triggers immediate key revocation and Guardianship intervention.
5. **Execution failure:** if the part-time coordinator (budget line 9) cannot be hired within 6 months of Tier 2 funding, or no municipal partner has signed within 12 months of campaign close, the wind-down path in item 1 executes.
6. **First assembly not fully fundable:** if the first full assembly cannot be fully funded by month 24, a reduced assembly (not below 100 residents) is drawn instead and the shortfall is published; the founder handover and sunset still execute on the original date.

## 7. What would halt this roadmap (honest list)

- Expert review (Phase 0) concluding that the transition premise is unsupported: halt, publish, return unspent funds.
- Three consecutive failed pilots: halt at replication, per the falsifier commitments in [02_Evidence_Base.md](02_Evidence_Base.md) and [07_Design_Rationale.md](07_Design_Rationale.md).
- A demonstrated ledger compromise without a clean migration: halt infrastructure, reassess substrate.
- Founder interference with expiry: the Guardians' first case, by design.
- Ratifiers voting for dissolution under the two-ballot rule ([01_Governance_Model.md](01_Governance_Model.md) §3, step 7): orderly sunset, full publication, keys destroyed on-ledger.

The roadmap's success metric is not "Chrysalis everywhere." It is: *the questions this protocol raises get answered deliberately, in public, reversibly, instead of by default.* Any phase that fails honestly advances that metric.
